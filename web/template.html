The same phone, listed three ways, at three prices. Which one is actually yours?

Marketplaces describe identical products differently and never agree on price, delivery or seller quality. This prototype reconciles listings across sources into single products, then ranks the competing offers against what an individual shopper actually cares about.

01

Ingestion

Every marketplace implements one SourceAdapter interface and normalises to a common Listing schema. Nothing downstream knows whether a listing arrived from a live API, a scraper, or the cache — which is what lets us add a source without touching the matching or ranking code.

02

Semantic entity resolution

Hard attributes are extracted by rule and enforced as constraints — two listings that disagree on storage, colour or model number can never merge. Within each block, titles are embedded and clustered by meaning. Open any product to see the offers that were joined and the attributes that were pulled out of their titles.

03

Multi-criteria ranking

Move the sliders. There is no single best offer — the winner depends entirely on the weight the shopper puts on price, speed and seller trust. Then switch the normalisation method to see the failure mode we found in the textbook formulation.

Matching — component ablation

Embeddings alone over-merge. "iPhone 15 128GB" and "iPhone 15 256GB" are about 97% cosine-similar but are different products — embeddings capture topic, not specification. Rules alone under-merge, failing on reordered titles like "Galaxy S24 5G 256 GB Black Samsung". Each stage fixes what the other cannot.

Ranking — normalisation comparison

Vector normalisation preserves each criterion's coefficient of variation. Across sellers, price varies a few percent while delivery time varies several-fold, so price ends up with roughly 22× less spread after normalisation. A weight of 0.8 on price then loses to a weight of 0.1 on delivery, and the shopper's slider stops changing anything.

What this evaluation does not yet show

The scores above come from a hand-labelled set of products. That is a smoke test confirming the approach is sound, not a publishable result. The next task is labelling 500–1000 listing pairs across two product categories, and the reported figures will move once real marketplace titles are included.
